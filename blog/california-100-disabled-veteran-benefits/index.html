California 100% Disabled Veteran Benefits (2026): The New Property Tax Law

If you are looking up California 100% disabled veteran benefits, start with the property tax exemption. Senate Bill 296 (Chapter 727, Statutes of 2026) changes it starting with the January 1, 2027 lien date. Below are today's amounts, the new law, and other benefits: DV plates, reduced-fee licenses, the Distinguished Veteran Pass, and the CalVet College Fee Waiver.

Today's disabled veterans' property tax exemption

California's Disabled Veterans' Exemption lowers property tax on the principal residence of veterans who, because of a service-connected injury or disease, are rated 100% disabled or compensated at the 100% rate due to unemployability. So TDIU counts. An unmarried surviving spouse may also claim it.

For 2026, BOE Letter to Assessors 2025/014 sets a basic exemption of $180,671 and a low-income exemption of $271,009, with a household income limit of $81,131. LTA 2026/019 already published the 2027 amounts under the old formula: basic $185,889, low-income $278,836, and a household income limit of $83,474.

The new law, starting with the 2027 lien date

SB 296 was approved September 28, 2026, and chaptered as Chapter 727, Statutes of 2026. For lien dates on or after January 1, 2027, and before January 1, 2032, it adds Revenue and Taxation Code §205.5.1, in lieu of §205.5 except as noted below. It exempts 50 percent of the part of your home's full value that does not exceed $1,000,000, a figure the law adjusts over time. This post does not state an adjusted 2027 cap, because BOE had not published that figure as of November 22, 2026.

Example only — your assessor figures the actual amount: a $600,000 home would have $300,000 exempt, compared with the old-formula 2027 basic amount of $185,889. If household income does not exceed $83,474, also adjusted, the exemption is on 100 percent of that first $1,000,000. Exception: if the home's full value is $371,778 or less, also adjusted, and household income is above that $83,474 limit, the old §205.5 exemption applies instead.

The home has to be owned by you, your spouse, or both, and be your principal residence. You have to be blind in both eyes, have lost the use of two or more limbs, or be totally disabled from an injury or disease incurred in military service. "Totally disabled" means rated 100 percent, or compensated at 100 percent because you cannot hold a substantially gainful occupation. The veteran definition also requires a discharge under other than dishonorable conditions; wartime service, a peacetime campaign or expedition with a Congressional medal, or a peacetime release for a service-connected disability; and that VA has found you eligible for federal veterans' health and medical benefits.

An unmarried surviving spouse can get the same amount if the veteran qualified during their lifetime or died from a VA-determined service-connected cause, lived in California on January 1 of the year they died, and the home was their principal residence at death. Give the assessor documentation, including a VA or branch letter showing the rating. This exemption is in place of any other real property tax exemption on that home. The BOE exemption page still did not mention SB 296 or §205.5.1 when re-checked on November 22, 2026, so ask your assessor how they are handling it.

How to file under today's rules — and what to ask about for 2027

Under the current exemption, the claim form is BOE-261-G, from your county assessor. For the full first-year exemption, file within 90 days of the qualifying event, or by the next January 1, whichever is later. File later, and you may get 85 percent for that first year. If a rating comes late, the current exemption can go back to the effective date, with taxes canceled or refunded subject to an eight-year limit in Revenue and Taxation Code §5097(a)(4), if you file within 90 days after you receive the rating, or by the next lien date, whichever is later. Those timing and refund rules are written for today's §205.5 exemption. SB 296 does not amend them, so ask your assessor whether the same form and deadlines apply under the new law.

DV license plates and parking

California's DV license plates exempt registration and license fees on one vehicle you own. You have to be permanently disabled from an injury or disease on active service, and either rated 100 percent for a condition that substantially impairs or interferes with mobility, unable to move without an assistive device, have lost the use of one or more limbs, or be permanently blind. Apply with Form REG 256V. With valid plates, you can park in wheelchair-symbol spaces, at blue curbs, at green curbs for as long as you wish, and at on-street meters at no charge.

Reduced-fee licenses and the Distinguished Veteran Pass

California Fish and Wildlife offers reduced-fee fishing and hunting licenses for honorably discharged disabled veterans with a 50 percent or greater service-connected disability, resident or nonresident. For 2026, the fishing license is $10.04 at a Fish and Wildlife office, or $10.54 from a license agent. CDFW has already posted 2027 fishing-license fees of $10.30 at a Fish and Wildlife office, or $10.80 from a license agent. The hunting license, for the July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027 license year, is $10.04 at a Fish and Wildlife office, or $10.54 from a license agent. Prequalify for the first one, then you can buy them anywhere licenses are sold.

The Distinguished Veteran Pass is free and covers vehicle day use, family camping, and boat use at over 100 state park units. You qualify if you are honorably discharged, a California resident, and have a combined service-connected rating of 50 percent or more, with service during wartime or operations against a foreign power. Former prisoners of war and Medal of Honor recipients qualify too.

College Fee Waiver (Plans A and B)

CalVet's College Fee Waiver waives mandatory systemwide tuition and fees at California community colleges, Cal State, and UC. It does not cover books, parking, or room and board. Sources: California Education Code §66025.3 and the DVS-40 application for 2026–27 (Riverside County Veterans Services).

Under Plan A, the veteran has to have served during a period of war, or earned a campaign or expeditionary medal, and be totally service-connected disabled. An unmarried child may qualify if they are over 14 and under 27, or under 30 if the child is a veteran; the spouse may qualify with no age limit. There is no income limit. The veteran's total rating had to come before the child's 21st birthday, and you cannot use Plan A and VA Chapter 35 at the same time.

Plan B may cover the child of a veteran with any service-connected disability if the child's income, counting parental support, does not exceed $22,941 for the 2025 tax year, which is the limit used for academic year 2026–27. Plan B can be used with Chapter 35. Students must meet the school's California residency rules. Apply with Form DVS-40 through your County Veterans Service Office.
