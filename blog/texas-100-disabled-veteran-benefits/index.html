Texas 100% Disabled Veteran Benefits (2026)

If you are looking up Texas 100% disabled veteran benefits, the big item is often property tax. Under Texas Tax Code §11.131, the state may exempt the total appraised value of your residence homestead. Texas also has disabled veteran plates, a toll waiver on TxDOT roads, a free driver's license, hunting and fishing, a state parks passport, and college hours for your family. Here is what each one takes, with official links. This is a summary, not a decision that you qualify.

Property tax: the 100% homestead exemption

Under Tax Code §11.131(b), a disabled veteran who VA has awarded 100 percent disability compensation for a service-connected disability, and a rating of 100 percent disabled or of individual unemployability, is entitled to an exemption from taxation of the total appraised value of the veteran's residence homestead. So TDIU counts here. The Comptroller's 100% disabled veteran FAQ says the veteran is eligible if he or she is either 100 percent disabled or is unemployable.

It applies only to your residence homestead, not every property you own.

To apply, file Form 50-114, the Residence Homestead Exemption Application, with the appraisal district where the property is. Do not file it with the Comptroller. The Comptroller says the filing deadline is April 30, but a 100 percent disabled veteran may file up to five years after the delinquency date for the taxes on the property. If you qualify after January 1, you may receive the exemption immediately for the applicable portion of that tax year.

Your surviving spouse can keep this exemption if they have not remarried, the property was their residence homestead at the veteran's death, and it remains their homestead — the Comptroller's surviving-spouse list, which matches §11.131.

There is a newer path too. Tax Code §11.136 covers the surviving spouse of a veteran who died as a result of a qualifying condition or disease, regardless of the veteran's rating at death. If that spouse has not remarried, they are entitled to an exemption of the total appraised value of their homestead. It is listed on the current Form 50-114 and took effect January 1, 2026 (HB 2508).

A separate exemption that can go on one other property

Texas also has a disabled veteran exemption under Tax Code §11.22. The Comptroller's disabled veteran FAQ says it can be applied to any one property the disabled veteran owns. At a 70 percent to 100 percent rating, the table on that page is $12,000 from the property's value.

The 100% FAQ says an eligible disabled veteran may receive both exemptions, and that the §11.22 exemption can be applied to any property the veteran owns, so this one can go on a second property. "Land or a rental" is only an example. Apply with Form 50-135 at the appraisal district where that property is.

Disabled veteran license plates

You can get disabled veteran plates if you have at least a 50 percent service-connected disability, or 40 percent because of the amputation of a lower extremity, and you receive compensation for it. Under Transportation Code §504.202, you may register one vehicle for your own use without the registration fees except the plate fee.

The state's form, VTR-615, says that plate fee is $3 a year for the first set. Extra vehicles may display DV plates, but registration and local fees apply. Apply with VTR-615 at your county tax assessor-collector's office, with a VA letter certifying your disability percentage (the form also allows a signed certification section).

Tolls on TxDOT roads

Since 2012, TxDOT has waived tolls for eligible veterans in vehicles registered in Texas that display a qualifying plate, including disabled veteran plates under Transportation Code §504.202. Other toll-road operators set their own rules, and some do not offer a discount. TxDOT's table lists which operators offer a disabled-veteran discount.

Free driver's license or ID, and a Disabled Veteran designation

If you have a service-related disability of at least 60 percent, an honorable discharge, and you receive disability compensation, you may qualify for a free Texas driver's license or ID card. The exemption does not apply to commercial driver's licenses. Show DPS a signed VA disability letter.

Veterans who are at least 50 percent disabled, or 40 percent disabled with a lower-extremity amputation, may also request a "DISABLED VETERAN" designation on the license.

Hunting, fishing, and state parks

Texas Parks and Wildlife offers the Disabled Veteran Super Combo for free. That package is a hunting license, a fishing license, and five state endorsements. You qualify with a disability rating of 50 percent or more, or the loss of use of a foot or leg, and you have to be receiving compensation for it. Each year, show VA proof that states your rate and was issued in the last 12 months, or a Texas driver's license with the Disabled Veteran designation. Get it in person at a license retailer. The federal duck stamp is not included.

The Disabled Veterans passport gives free entry to Texas state parks for a veteran with a 60 percent or more service-connected disability, or loss of a lower extremity, and a companion gets in free too. Bring a valid photo ID and, as that page lists, a VA award letter, a disabled veteran plate receipt, or a tax exemption letter for Texas veterans.

College: the Hazlewood Act

The Hazlewood Act gives qualified veterans, spouses, and dependent children up to 150 hours of tuition exemption, including most fees, at Texas public colleges. It does not cover books, supply fees, or living expenses.

If you entered the service in Texas, or lived in Texas when you entered, and VA rates you 100 percent permanent and total or awards individual unemployability, your spouse and each dependent child may qualify for their own 150 hours. The student has to be classified as a Texas resident, and other school rules apply. See the Texas Veterans Commission's Hazlewood page.

Where to get help

The Texas Veterans Commission has accredited claims benefit advisors around the state and offers its services for free. An accredited VSO representative can also help with your VA claim for free: va.gov/get-help-from-accredited-representative.
